# Origo — What We Are Building, and Why

> Shareable alignment doc. If you're joining the team, contributing code, or
> promoting Origo on campus — read this first. Everything we build should be
> defensible in one sentence, and this is that sentence.

---

## The one sentence

> **Origo is how you find your people on campus.**

Not "meet strangers." Not "date." **Find your people** — the ones you'd have
found eventually, three semesters later, by accident. Origo compresses that.

---

## Who it's for, right now

Students at **BITS Pilani, Hyderabad Campus** — starting with the incoming
batch, during the one week a year when an entire cohort arrives knowing nobody
and desperately wants to.

We expand campus by campus, and only after one campus is genuinely dense.
Being everywhere thinly is how campus apps die.

---

## What Origo is not

**Not a dating app.** This is the most important line in this document.

Romance is one outcome Origo supports. It is not the product. The moment
campus perception codes us as a dating app, the majority of students — who
would never install one — are gone. **And they are the network.** Without
them there is no product for anyone, including the people who did want to date.

This is a hard constraint on what we build, not a marketing preference:

- Romantic discovery is **opt-in and episodic** (Radar moments tied to real
  campus events), never the default surface.
- The default experience is group-shaped: your batch, your communities, what's
  happening tonight.
- We never lead with dating in any copy, poster, or pitch.

**Not anonymous.** Every feature is identity-attached. Anonymous campus apps
(YikYak and its descendants) reliably become harassment platforms and get banned
from campuses. Our entire premise is verified, accountable identity. There is no
anonymous posting anywhere in Origo and there never will be.

**Not a feed to scroll.** We are not competing for idle attention. Origo
succeeds when you close it and go meet someone.

## Why we win

Every alternative a fresher already has is broken in a specific way:

| Alternative | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| **Instagram freshers pages** | Anonymous admins, open DMs, publicly scrapeable posts, zero control for the person featured, dead by week two. |
| **WhatsApp batch groups** | 300 unread messages, no profiles, no discovery, the loudest ten people dominate, unverified randoms circulate. |
| **Tinder U / dating apps** | Dating-only positioning and dating-app stigma. No communities, no events, no campus moments. A global company that will never staff a stall at your orientation. |
| **Discord / Reddit servers** | Pseudonymous — fine for memes, useless for actually meeting someone. |

Our moat isn't the verification checkbox — others can copy that. It's the
**community layer plus the ground game**: we are *from* this campus, we are at
orientation, and we run the campus calendar. That doesn't port.

## The product, and how each piece ladders to the sentence

Three tabs. Ambient first, one-to-one second, conversations third.

### Campus — always populated
| Surface | How it finds your people |
|---|---|
| **Batch Space** | The room your whole batch is already in. Populated by default — nobody has to find anybody. |
| **Pulse** | "I'm at the canteen, who's around?" Find people *right now*, for something specific. Expires in 3 hours, so it's never stale. |
| **Happening** | Know where people will be. During orientation this should simply be the best campus schedule that exists. |
| **Ask a Senior** | The questions freshers actually have, answered by people who know. |

### People — deliberate, governed
| Surface | How it finds your people |
|---|---|
| **Discover** | People like you — same batch, branch, hometown, interests. Surfaced through shared context, not ranked by looks. |
| **Ship a Friend** | Your friends connect you. The most on-brand feature we have: finding your people *through* your people. |
| **Rizz In 5** | Five messages to make it count. Scarcity forces effort and gives shy people a script. |

### Chats
Where connections actually live once they exist.

### Intro Card — the growth loop
A genuinely good-looking shareable card with a QR to your profile. It works
with **zero other users on the app** — you get something valuable alone, the
network makes it better. Posted to a story, it's an ad with a link on it.

## Principles we hold to

1. **Empty is worse than absent.** A surface nobody is on signals a dead app.
   We ship fewer things that feel alive rather than more that feel abandoned.
2. **Density before breadth.** One dense campus beats five thin ones. One dense
   batch beats a thin campus.
3. **Consent by construction.** People should only receive contact they invited.
   Opt-in romantic discovery, capacity limits, declines that stick.
4. **Safety is existential, not a feature.** One incident involving a fresher,
   amplified through the same WhatsApp groups we grow through, could end the
   campus before it starts.
5. **We optimise for connections made, not time spent.** If someone opens Origo,
   finds a person, and closes it — that is a win.

## The ten-second pitch

> "You know how you spend your first year randomly bumping into people and only
> find your actual friends by second year? Origo is your campus — your batch,
> clubs, what's happening tonight — so you find them in week one instead.
> Everyone on it is a verified student."

Note what that pitch does **not** contain: the word "dating," the word "app,"
and any mention of an algorithm.

---

## How we'll know it's working

- Share of active users who make **at least one real connection in their first
  two weeks.** This is the number that matters. Not installs, not sessions.
- Whether people say *"we met on Origo."* Word of mouth is a design output —
  every feature should end in a story someone tells.
